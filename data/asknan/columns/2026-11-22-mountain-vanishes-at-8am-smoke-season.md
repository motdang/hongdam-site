Dear Lost Mountain,

March. Satellites counted 1.82 million fires across the north and its neighbours from 2003 to 2024. 46% of them burned in March, and 90% between February and April [1]. The busiest day, on average, is 30 March [1]. Your neighbour is right about the mornings too. The reason is a lid.

## The valley wears a lid at breakfast

Chiang Mai sits in a bowl of mountains. On a clear night in the dry season, the ground cools fast. The air on the valley floor ends up colder than the air above it. Cold air is heavy, so it stays down, and the smoke stays with it [1].

In March that lid is about 68 m above the city at 7:00. Then the sun warms the ground, the warm air rises, and the lid lifts. By 15:00 it is about 1,822 m up [1]. The smoke has more room, so each breath of air has less of it. Halve the height of the lid and the smoke doubles [1].

The smoke is counted as PM2.5: particles under 2.5 thousandths of a millimetre, small enough to reach deep in the lungs [1]. The unit is µg/m³, micrograms in one cubic metre of air.

| Hour, average March day | PM2.5 in Chiang Mai |
|---|---|
| Around 08:00 | 48 µg/m³, the highest of the day |
| Around 15:00 | 28 µg/m³, the lowest of the day |

The air 800 m up is warmer than the ground on 68% of March mornings at 7:00. At 15:00 that is true on 1% of days [1].

## The mountain is a meter

From Tha Phae Gate, Doi Suthep's ridge is 10.8 km to the west. It fades from view at about 26 µg/m³ [1]. At a March breakfast, with 48 in the air, it is gone. On an afternoon when the number falls under 26, it comes back.

## Some years are much smokier

Thailand's limit is 37.5 µg/m³ over 24 hours. It took effect on 1 June 2023; before that it was 50 [2]. These are the days over 37.5 from January to April [1]:

| Town | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chiang Mai | 40 | 8 | 29 | 45 |
| Chiang Rai | 44 | 18 | 36 | 51 |

The counts come from CAMS, a European forecast of the air on a 40 km grid. It smooths the worst hours. On 29 March 2026, stations in Chiang Mai read 188 µg/m³, and the model had 47 for the day [1]. The Pollution Control Department's stations report at air4thai.com [1].

## Most of the fires are across the border

The Mot Dang map runs from Myanmar's Shan State to northern Laos. Of the fires in it, 39% burned in Myanmar, 36% in Laos and 24% in Thailand [1]. Inside northern Thailand, forest burns more than farmland. From January to May 2024 the Thai agency GISTDA counted 1.83 million rai of burned forest and 533,000 rai of burned cornfields. A rai is a Thai unit of land. That is about three to one [1].

NASA describes the mix: crop fires in the valleys, forest fires in the mountains, and the city's own traffic and industry. It collects in the Ping River valley, where Chiang Mai sits [3].

Chiang Mai banned open burning from 1 January to 31 May 2026. A burn inside the ban is registered in a system called FireD, and the district gives or refuses permission [4].

## The rain ends it

Rain stops around November and comes back around May. By March, almost anything dry will burn [1]. When the rain returns, PM2.5 drops to between 5 and 10 µg/m³ [1][3]. From the Doi Suthep lookout in the rainy season, the hills of Doi Saket show 25 km away [1].

Look west at 3 p.m. in March. If Doi Suthep is there, the lid has lifted.
