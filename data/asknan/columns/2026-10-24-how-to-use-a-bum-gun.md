The head sits about a hand's width away and the squeeze is light. The hose beside a Thai toilet is สายฉีดชำระ (*sai chit chamra*, "spray hose for cleansing"). The lever under its head sets the force, and a quarter squeeze is a soft spray [1].

On 11 October 2026 I asked for a "cleverly illustrated minisite" on how to use one, and to "add lots of toys". The result has a tappable bathroom, a game, a parts toy you take apart, and the eight steps below [1]. Its squeeze toy sets out the range: too soft dribbles on the floor, too hard reaches the gecko on the ceiling [1].

## Eight steps, in the order the page draws them

1. The small valve on the wall opens. It feeds only the gun.
2. One short squirt goes into the bowl. It shows how hard this gun sprays and how warm the water is.
3. The reach is from behind, or down between the knees. People do both.
4. The squeeze is gentle, the head about a hand's width away.
5. The spray passes front to back, two or three times.
6. The gun hangs back on its clip.
7. A few sheets of paper pat dry.
8. The valve closes.

A how-to guide written by foreigners, quoted by Bangkok Biznews, also says to try a squirt first, because pressure and temperature differ from place to place [2].

## The valve on the wall sets the force

The stop valve is the small tap on the supply pipe that shuts the water to the gun. HomePro's HomeGuru describes a spray that arrives hard enough to make people flinch at a public or unfamiliar toilet, and names the stop valve as the control: twisted, it sets the pressure that reaches the head [3]. On the page, a half-open valve softens a strong spray [1].

A HANG set at HomePro lists a flow of no more than 6 litres a minute and pressure up to 1 bar, a unit slightly below the average air pressure at sea level [5]. It costs ฿169, read on 24 October 2026 [4]. Full sets in the page's price table run from ฿169 to ฿615, read on 10 October 2026 [1].

## Where the paper goes

The sign on the wall says bin or bowl, and many Thai toilets keep a bin for used paper [1]. In 2009 the newspaper Manager tested tissue brands, found that two-ply paper (two layers) clumps when wet, and wrote that paper belongs outside the toilet and the drains [6]. In the page's bin-or-bowl game, wet wipes, pads, cotton buds, plastic and hair go in the bin, and a dipper of water goes in the bowl [1].

## Squat toilets and the dipper

In a 2010 household survey, 86.0% of Thai homes had a squat toilet, 10.1% a sitting one and 3.1% both [7]. Wikipedia says "the 'bum gun' is ubiquitous" in Thailand, and that a Thai bathroom usually also has a plastic dipper, ขัน (*khan*), used in bathing [8]. A squat pan with no cistern (water tank) flushes by pouring (ส้วมราดน้ำ, *suam rat nam*); the page's toy counts three dipperfuls [1].

## A little history

A Thai firm, now the brand HANG, says it began making spray sets in 1972 and has made over 50 million [2]. That is the company's claim; Sanook repeats it and adds that Thailand did not invent the hose [9]. The Finnish firm Oras made a hand shower for the toilet, the Bidetta, in 1968 [10].

The names: สายฉีดก้น (*sai chit kon*, "bottom spray hose") is the joking one [9]. ที่ฉีดตูด (*thi chit tut*) is slang [11]. "Bum gun" is English slang, and Wiktionary lists "health faucet" among its English synonyms [11]. The word ชำระ (*chamra*) means to cleanse, and the same word pays a bill: ชำระเงิน (*chamra ngoen*) [1].

On 15 August 2019 an MP raised that the toilets in the new parliament building had no สายฉีดชำระ, and the House Speaker went to inspect them [12].

## Paper, counted

A person in the US goes through 141 rolls of toilet paper a year; in the UK 127, in Japan 91, in France 71 (Reuters, 2019, on a report by two environmental groups) [13]. A Thai figure in rolls was not found.

## A gun that drips

HomeGuru lists the causes: a cracked head, a broken lever, a loose joint, a leaking hose, or water pressure too high. Pressure is the most common, in dorms, condos and houses with a strong pump, where the head shakes when it is used. Its fix for the pressure case is the stop valve, turned down, and shut when the gun sits idle for a long time [3].
