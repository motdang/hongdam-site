Dear Can't Sleep,

Your house is not haunted. Your pump has one of two faults, and the time it clicks tells you which. Calpeda is a pump maker with an office in Bangkok. It splits the faults the same way. One pump starts by itself while nobody uses water. The other starts and stops fast while a tap is open [2].

## How the pump knows when to start

A pressure-tank pump, like the Mitsubishi WP or the Hitachi WM-P, has a tank of air under pressure. In Thai it is the ถังแรงดัน (*thang raeng dan*, pressure tank). A tap opens, and the air pushes water out. The pressure falls to a low mark, and a switch starts the pump. The tap shuts, the pressure climbs to a high mark, and the pump stops [1]. For Mitsubishi WP pumps, a dealer lists the two marks as 1.4 and 1.9 kg/cm². That means kilograms per square centimetre, a unit of pressure [6].

## Clicks with every tap shut: a leak

A leak lets the pressure fall slowly. When it reaches the low mark, the pump starts again. In Calpeda's example, that happens every 5, 10 or 30 minutes [2]. Hitachi's Thai site gives the same first check: every tap shut, then a look for leaks at taps, pipes and the toilet [3].

Calpeda's test [2]:

1. The gate valve on the pipe from the pump into the house is closed. The Mot Dang page closes it for 1 to 2 minutes [1].
2. The pump stops: the leak is inside the house. Calpeda names the toilet's float valve, the bum gun (the spray hose beside the toilet), taps, the water heater, and pipe joints under the sink or above the ceiling.
3. The pump keeps clicking: the leak is at the pump or in the short pipe before the valve. Or a worn check valve, a one-way valve, lets water run back.

A few drops of food colouring find a toilet leak. They go in the cistern, the tank behind the seat. Colour in the bowl with no flush means the float or the rubber flap leaks [1]. A leak inside a wall shows as damp marks, swollen or peeling paint, or drips from the ceiling [5].

## Clicks fast while a tap runs: the tank lost its air

Here the pump runs for a second or two, stops, and starts again. It happens mostly while a tap runs gently. Calpeda writes that this is usually a pressure-tank fault. The common one is too little air. A small use of water drops the pressure to the low mark at once. Closing the tap sends it straight to the high mark [2].

The makers' fixes:

- **Hitachi round tank.** Toyobo is Hitachi's official Thai distributor. Its steps: the pump is switched off, and the valves in and out are closed. The plug under the tank opens until the water runs out. Then the plug closes and the pump is switched on. Still clicking after that? A blocked air-intake part may need replacing, or a technician [4].
- **Hitachi square tank.** With the power and valves off, Toyobo shakes the pressure tank. Water leaking out means the tank has failed and is replaced [4].
- **A tank with an air valve.** Calpeda drains the system and pumps air in to the maker's figure. That is usually 2 to 3 psi below the pump's start pressure. Psi means pounds per square inch. Water coming out with the air means the rubber bag inside is torn [2].

## A weak shower

Weak at one shower or tap: scale, a hard crust from the water, sits in the shower head or the aerator. The aerator is the small screen at the tip of a tap. Both unscrew for rinsing. Weak everywhere: a clogged filter cartridge, or more taps open than the pump can feed [1]. HomeGuru, the advice pages of the Thai shop HomePro, names air in the pump as a common cause. Opening the plug on the pump body and filling it with water clears it [5]. A house on mains water with no pump reports weak flow to the Provincial Waterworks Authority on 1662. Its manual sets 24 hours for fixing weak or stop-start flow [10].
