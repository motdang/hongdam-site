Dear Six Hours Early,

Your friend was on time. สองทุ่ม (*song thum*) means 8 p.m., not 2 o'clock. Thailand runs two clocks at once. On paper it uses the 24-hour clock. Out loud, most people use an older count of six-hour blocks [1].

## Four blocks, and each one starts again at 1

The spoken clock cuts the day into four blocks of six hours. In each block the count starts again at one. Each block has its own word [1][2]:

| Clock | Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01:00–05:00 | ตี (*ti*) | strike | ตีสอง (*ti song*) = 2 a.m. |
| 06:00–11:00 | โมงเช้า (*mong chao*) | gong, morning | แปดโมงเช้า (*paet mong chao*) = 8 a.m. |
| 13:00–18:00 | บ่าย…โมง (*bai … mong*) | afternoon, gong | บ่ายสองโมง (*bai song mong*) = 2 p.m. |
| 19:00–23:00 | ทุ่ม (*thum*) | drum | สองทุ่ม (*song thum*) = 8 p.m. |

Noon is เที่ยง (*thiang*). Midnight is เที่ยงคืน (*thiang khuen*), "the noon of the night" [1].

So the number two can mean 2 a.m., 2 p.m. or 8 p.m. The word next to the number names the block. The wrong block puts a person six hours early, or twelve hours late [1]. The shop opens at บ่ายโมง: 1 p.m.

Two small extras. At 4 p.m. people say บ่ายสี่โมง (*bai si mong*). Many also say สี่โมงเย็น, "4 gongs, evening". 5 p.m. works the same way [1][2]. And an older way counts the morning from one as well: สองโมงเช้า (*song mong chao*) for 8 a.m. The 12-hour way, แปดโมงเช้า, is the one heard more often today [2].

## A gong by day, a drum by night

Before clocks, keepers struck a gong (ฆ้อง, *khong*) through the day. Through the night they beat a drum (กลอง, *klong*). People named the hour by the sound they heard. โมง is the boom of the gong. ทุ่ม is the thud of the drum. ตี is the verb "strike" [1][3].

At 6 a.m. and 6 p.m. the keepers beat a long run of strokes, called ย่ำ (*yam*). Dawn was ย่ำรุ่ง (*yam rung*). Dusk was ย่ำค่ำ (*yam kham*) [1][2]. Thai Wikipedia gives both หกโมงเย็น and ย่ำค่ำ for 6 p.m. [3].

## The king's list of 1900

In July 1900, the Royal Gazette (the Thai government's official paper) printed a list of hour names for King Chulalongkorn, Rama V. It said people were using some names wrongly. It named five forms to stop: 6 โมงเช้า, 6 โมงเย็น, 3 ทุ่ม, 6 ทุ่ม and 9 ทุ่ม [1]. English Wikipedia gives the year as 1901 [2]. The gazette's own date, Rattanakosin year 119, is 1900 [1].

Today people say all five of the forms the list struck out [1].

## On paper: นาฬิกา and a coconut

Written down, and on the radio, Thai time uses the 24-hour clock. A time is written with น., short for นาฬิกา (*nalika*), "clock": 14.30 น. is 2.30 p.m. [1].

นาฬิกา first meant a coconut. Siam kept time with half a coconut shell with a small hole in the bottom. It floated in a basin of water. When the shell filled and sank, one hour had passed [1].

## Half past, and ten more minutes

Half past is ครึ่ง (*khrueng*). บ่ายสองโมงครึ่ง (*bai song mong khrueng*) is 2.30 p.m. Other minutes take the number and นาที (*nathi*), minutes. Near the hour, people count down. อีกสิบนาทีสามทุ่ม, "ten more minutes to three drums", is 8.50 p.m. [1].

The Telling Time in Thai page has a dial. Drag the hand, and it writes the time the way people say it. It plays a gong for โมง and a drum for ทุ่ม. Its eight-question quiz uses the usual mix-ups as the wrong answers: the same number in another block [1].

After dark, every hour is a drum. Two drums: 8 p.m., and a friend at the café.
