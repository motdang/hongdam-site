Dear Guessing Again,

There is a better way, and it comes from a parking meter. Write a list of round prices once, like the steps of a ladder. Then let your orders move the price one step at a time.

## The professor who priced parking

Donald Shoup is a professor of city planning at UCLA, the University of California, Los Angeles. He wrote *The High Cost of Free Parking* in 2005 and updated it in 2011 [1][2]. His idea: a city charges a fair market price for parking on the street. Then it spends the money on services in the same neighborhood [1].

A 2003 article in ACCESS Magazine gave the test. Too many empty spaces? The price goes down. Too few? The price goes up [3].

## San Francisco tried it

From August 2011, San Francisco, in the USA, tested this on its parking meters. Its transport agency, SFMTA, changed the price on each street block about every 8 weeks. It looked at occupancy: the share of spaces with a car in them [4].

| Spaces in use | Next hourly price |
|---|---|
| 80 to 100% | up US$0.25 |
| 60 to 80% | no change |
| 30 to 60% | down US$0.25 |
| under 30% | down US$0.50 |

The price stayed between US$0.25 and US$6.00 an hour. In the two-year trial the agency changed prices 10 times. The average price fell from US$2.69 to US$2.58 [4].

## The money stays on the street

Old Pasadena is a district of Pasadena, California, USA. It had no parking meters until 1993, and its shop owners did not want them. So the city made an offer: all the meter money would be spent in Old Pasadena. The business owners and property owners said yes [3]. In the 2001 financial year, its 690 meters earned US$1.2 million after costs. US$448,000 of that paid back a US$5 million loan for sidewalks and alleys [3].

## Your ladder, in baht

A ladder is a list of allowed prices. The lowest price is the floor. The highest is the ceiling. The price sits on one step. At each check it moves one step, or it stays.

This example is made up. A service can take 10 customers a month. Its ladder is ฿1,000, ฿1,200, ฿1,500, ฿2,000 and ฿2,500. It starts at ฿1,500.

| Orders last month | Share of the 10 places | Price next month |
|---|---|---|
| 9 or 10 | over 80% | ฿2,000 |
| 6 to 8 | 60 to 80% | ฿1,500 |
| 0 to 5 | under 60% | ฿1,200 |

At ฿2,500 the price cannot go higher. At ฿1,000 it cannot go lower. Each step on this ladder changes the price by 20 to 33%.

A Thai ladder can use nines. The Thailand Foundation says nine, เก้า (*kao*), is the luckiest number in Thai belief. It sounds like ก้าว (*kao*), "to move forward" [7].

## Hongdam prices this way

On 15 October 2026 I said: "Everywhere that we can do Shoup for pricing, I'd like to follow that model. It's a universal preference for me." For Hongdam I described it as "prices set by the market itself. Round or auspicious numbers, generous on both sides. Rarely determined by me." Auspicious means lucky.

Hongdam's price list states the rule [5]. Each service has slots, the places it has each month. The check runs on the 1st of each month at 00:00, Bangkok time. Orders over 80% of the slots: one step up. Under 60%: one step down. In between: no change. Each service stays between its own floor and ceiling. The list shows 1 December 2026 as the first check [5].

A service card in Hongdam's shop shows the ladder as a row of bars, with the current step marked. A line says how many places are left, such as "4 places left this month" [6]. On 24 October 2026 the house or land check read ฿8,000 now, ฿5,000 low and ฿15,000 high. Its 6 steps are ฿5,000, ฿6,000, ฿8,000, ฿10,000, ฿12,000 and ฿15,000. They come from one long ladder of 36 numbers, from ฿10 to ฿200,000 [6].

On the same day, "Paper forms typed into a sheet" had 10 slots. Its floor was ฿500, its ceiling ฿2,000 and its price ฿1,000 [5]. By the rule, 9 or 10 orders in a month move it one step up. 5 or fewer move it one step down.

Hongdam also sells this system. Its card "Prices that follow demand" sets prices for rooms, a menu, tickets or stock. It works "the way San Francisco prices parking" [8].

So next month's price is not a guess. Your customers choose it, one step at a time.
