Dear Locked Out,

The claim itself needs no sign-in. A surviving spouse of a US veteran can claim on paper, by post [1][2]. The form is VA Form 21P-534EZ, Application for D.I.C., Survivors Pension, and/or Accrued Benefits. It goes to the Department of Veterans Affairs, Pension Intake Center, PO Box 5365, Janesville, WI 53547-5365 [1][2]. VA is the US Department of Veterans Affairs.

D.I.C. means Dependency and Indemnity Compensation. It is a tax-free benefit for the family left behind, in two cases. In the first, the veteran died of a service-connected illness or injury, one caused by military service. In the second, VA had rated the veteran totally disabled for a set period [1].

## Four things go by post

- **The claim.** If VA receives it within one year after the death, the benefit starts from the first day of the month of the death [2]. An Intent to File, VA Form 21-0966, keeps that date while you gather papers [1][2].
- **The report of the death.** By post, it goes to the Claims Intake Center, PO Box 4444, Janesville, WI 53547-4444. VA says a phone call is fastest. A posted report may take longer to stop the veteran's payments [3].
- **Social Security.** For Thailand, US Social Security names the Federal Benefits Unit at the US Embassy in the Philippines: 1201 Roxas Boulevard, Ermita, Manila 0930, Philippines [4].
- **Health insurance.** CHAMPVA is a VA program that shares the cost of care. It covers the surviving spouse in two cases: the veteran died of a service-connected disability, or was rated permanently and totally disabled at death. VA Form 10-10d goes to VHA Office of Community Care, CHAMPVA Eligibility, PO Box 137, Spring City, PA 19475 [5]. VA's page does not say how it works outside the US.

## Why the sign-in keeps failing

VA accepts three sign-in accounts: ID.me, Login.gov or CLEAR. Each one checks who you are [6]. Login.gov asks for a US driver's license, a state ID or a US passport book. CLEAR asks for a Social Security number [6]. These are US papers. For an address outside the US, VA names ID.me or CLEAR. On ID.me you choose "I don't live in the US". Then you show your documents to an ID.me agent on a video call [7].

ID.me has a page for people outside the US who have a US Social Security number. It asks for a document that shows all nine digits of the number. It also asks for two primary documents, or one primary and one secondary. A passport counts as a primary document. The page says to choose "I don't live in the United States", not Self-Service or Video Call. If the international phone number shows an error, it points to a way that needs no calls or texts [8].

Without a Social Security number, VA's page lists a valid US passport as one of the documents [7]. ID.me has a separate page for a non-US passport and proof of address [9]. These pages do not say which route fits a widow with a Thai passport. Nobody at Hongdam has tried that route.

You do not have to sit alone on the video call. ID.me lets an interpreter in the room, a family member or a technology helper join. The helper appears on screen at the start. The helper cannot answer for you [10].

## A free guide in Thai

On 20 October 2026 I described my tech-support sessions for US veterans and Thai-speaking widows. The work means a husband's passwords and personal data on old devices, in a language the widow does not read. I have never finished one in a single session. So I asked for a free, thorough minisite, a small website, in all the languages. A widow, widower or veteran could use it to sign in to these services. It carries the line "keep this handy, share with a friend".

That guide is Getting In, in 12 languages [11]. The Thai version is เข้าบัญชีให้ได้ (khâo ban-chii hâi dâai). It was checked against official pages on 20 October 2026 [12]. It plans for two sessions from the start. It has a checklist for the first one, and ID.me from outside the US, step by step [11].

It lists the places where the sign-in fails, and the way past each one [11]:

- a VPN, a tool that changes where your connection seems to come from, which triggers error 1015;
- iPhone photos in HEIC, an Apple picture format that ID.me refuses;
- Thai dates in the Buddhist Era, where the year is 543 higher;
- a name spelled two ways on two papers.

The guide notes that the veteran's accounts belong to him. A surviving spouse needs an account of their own [11].

It also lists the signs of someone who only pretends to help. They contact you first, want money before filing, or ask for a password or the code sent to your phone [11]. ID.me's own page says "ID.me will also never ask for your password or MFA information" [13]. MFA, short for multi-factor authentication, is the extra sign-in code.
