Dear Hungry,

The number on your pack is a button, not a number of minutes. Press 4 is not 4 minutes either. In 2021 a sweet potato burned because of that mistake.

## The burned sweet potato

On 10 June 2021, someone wrote on Pantip, a Thai discussion forum. A shop clerk had told them that กด 4 meant 4 minutes. So they heated a Japanese sweet potato for 4 minutes, and it burned. An account named น้องเปาเซเว่น (Nong Pao 7-Eleven) replied. A normal microwave takes about 1 to 1½ minutes, it wrote, and the pack has more information [8].

## กด 5 is a button on the shop's microwave

กด (*kot*) means "press". The number on a ready meal is a program saved in the shop's microwave. The staff press one number. The oven then sets its own power and time [3].

Postjung, a Thai online forum, described this oven on 23 April 2021. It is stainless steel, made for heavy use, and weighs over 30 kg. Its keys are numbered 1 to 0 in place of watts, and the brand is Panasonic [4]. Watts (W) measure an oven's power. Thai reports do not agree on the model [3].

## One number, two shop times

The pack names two shop oven powers, so one number gives two times. Times here are minutes and seconds: 1:10 is 1 minute 10 seconds. 7-Eleven's online shop lists the Kurobuta pork curry rice like this [5]:

- 1,800 W, press 6: 1:10
- 1,300 W, press 6: 1:30
- a home oven at 800 W: 1:45 to 2:00

How cold the food is matters too. Sipso rice soup is press 5 at room temperature. From the fridge, at 2 to 6 °C, it is press 6 [6]. Frozen meals use 7 or 8, and a second press [3]. Two presses, like 6 and 2, do not double the time. The second press adds a short program, about 15 to 20 seconds in the shop [3].

## Your microwave at home

The Wave It at Home page turns each code into home minutes. At 800 W, press 5 is 1:30 to 2:00. Press 6 is 1:45 to 2:40 [3].

Two packs print their own home time. The curry prints 1:45 to 2:00 for code 6 [5]. Sipso prints 1:00 to 1:30 at 800 W for code 5 [6].

Your oven's watts are on a sticker inside the door, or on the back [3]. A small home microwave of 17 to 23 litres uses 700 to 900 W [7].

## The home times are worked out, not published

Nobody has found a published table of each code's power and time [3]. So the page calculates them itself, and the times are not 7-Eleven's figures. Watts times minutes is the heat the food takes in. The shop times give the heat for each code.

A pack line that says "under 1,300 W" is read as 800 W. That is the middle of what the packs suggest: about 650 to 1,050 W. From code 2 to code 6, the heat rises about 1.46 times each step. Code 1 is estimated from that pattern, with no pack seen. Codes 7 and 8 come from frozen packs [3].

One test: Creator sea bass rice soup lists 800 W for 5:00 and 1,300 W for 3:00. The two agree within 3% [3]. The page also lists what it does not know. The AB button appears on one pack only. No pack prints a time for code 1, or for code 8 alone [3].

## Why Thailand loves 7-Eleven

CP All, the company that runs 7-Eleven in Thailand, had 16,284 stores at the end of June 2026. It opened 200 of them from April to June [1].

Mot Dang, Hongdam's online directory at motdang.net, lists 487 7-Elevens in Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai. 92 have a cash machine. 231 are open all night, a little under half. That count is from 18 September 2026 [2].

On 26 September 2026 I removed a line that called all 7-Elevens the same. My note: "That's not true, 7-Elevens are sacred." The rest of the feeling: "hello welcome", popsicles and beer.

The One Man Sideshow page has a banner, "7-Elevens are sacred". It shows สวัสดีค่ะ (*sawatdee kha*, hello), เบียร์เย็น (*bia yen*, cold beer) and ไอติม (*ai tim*, popsicles) [2]. Wave It at Home opens with the shop's greeting: สวัสดีค่ะ เซเว่นอีเลฟเว่น ยินดีต้อนรับค่ะ (*sawatdee kha, seven-eleven, yindi tonrap kha*: hello, welcome). Its list puts popsicles and beer under "No wave. Open the fridge door and take one." [3]

The popsicles and the beer need no button at all.
