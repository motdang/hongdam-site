Useful facts

  • The land office charges 2% to register a transfer. The rate is applied to the appraised value (ราคาประเมินทุนทรัพย์). dol.go.th
  • A specific business tax of 3.3% applies to a sale within 5 years of acquiring the property, among other listed cases. It is worked out on the appraised value or the sale price, whichever is higher. A sale after 5 years is among the exemptions on the page. dol.go.th
  • Stamp duty is ฿1 for every ฿200 of value, or part of ฿200 (0.5%), on a transfer made for payment. dol.go.th
  • The seller's income tax is withheld on the sale. For an individual, net income is divided by the years held (10 at most; a part year counts as one), taxed at the progressive rates, and multiplied back by the years. For a company the rate is 1% of the sale price or the appraised value, whichever is higher. dol.go.th
  • The Department of Lands lists the ways a foreigner can acquire land, among them a home under section 96 bis of the Land Code and inheritance as a statutory heir under section 93. dol.go.th
  • Foreigners, and companies the law treats as foreign, may together hold up to 49% of the unit area in a condominium building, measured when the building is registered (section 19 bis of the Condominium Act). dol.go.th
  • Section 19 of the Condominium Act names who may hold a unit: a foreigner permitted to reside under immigration law, a foreigner admitted under investment promotion law, and a foreigner who brings in foreign currency or draws on a foreign-currency or non-resident baht account. dol.go.th

What people ask

Can a foreigner buy land in Thailand?

The Department of Lands lists the ways a foreigner can acquire land. They include land for a home under section 96 bis of the Land Code and inheritance as a statutory heir under section 93. A Thai with a foreign spouse, and a company in an investment-promoted project, have their own entries on the list. dol.go.th

Can a foreigner own a condominium unit?

Yes, within a limit. Foreigners, and companies the law treats as foreign, may together hold up to 49% of the unit area in a building (section 19 bis of the Condominium Act). Section 19 names who may hold, among them a foreigner permitted to reside under immigration law and a foreigner who brings in foreign currency. dol.go.th

Does a land document show burdens on the plot?

Yes, it can carry entries for burdens on the plot (ภาระผูกพัน). When a chanote is issued in place of an older document, section 59 quater of the Land Code has those entries, and any change of the right-holder during the issue, copied into it. comment.ocs.go.th

What is the assessed price (ราคาประเมิน) used for?

The land office applies its 2% transfer fee to the appraised value (ราคาประเมินทุนทรัพย์). The 3.3% specific business tax uses the appraised value or the sale price, whichever is higher. dol.go.th

Buying a second-hand house: which fees and taxes are on the sale?

The Department of Lands' page lists a 2% transfer fee, stamp duty of ฿1 per ฿200, and a 3.3% specific business tax for a seller who acquired the property within 5 years. It also sets out the seller's withholding income tax. dol.go.th

Can a Nor Sor 3 Gor be changed into a chanote?

In the Department of Lands' survey project, an NS3 ก. on rice-field land is replaced by a chanote at no cost and without a boundary survey (รังวัดปักหลักเขต). The NS3 ก. is cancelled when the chanote is handed out. dol.go.th

Is a Sor Kor 1 (ส.ค.1) a title deed?

No. The Department of Lands calls it a notice of possession from before 1954, not the ownership certificate (โฉนด) the state issues. A holder can use it to apply for a chanote, and since 9 February 2010 a court order confirming the right comes first. dol.go.th

Who can take over a Sor Por Kor 4-01 plot?

The Bueng Kan land reform office lists a spouse, an adult child, a parent, a brother or sister, a grandchild, and a brother's or sister's child. The permit is for working land in a land reform area. The same list applies when the holder dies. alro.go.th
