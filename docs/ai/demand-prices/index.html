Good to know

  • San Francisco's SFpark set a target of 60–80% occupancy for on-street meters. When average occupancy was 80–100%, the hourly rate rose US$0.25. At 60–80% it did not change. At 30–60% it fell US$0.25, and under 30% it fell US$0.50. sfmta.com
  • Hourly meter rates in SFpark could not go above US$6.00 or below US$0.25. They changed about every eight weeks from August 2011, and the city made ten changes through June 2013. sfmta.com
  • For city garages, San Francisco changed hourly rates every three months. At 80–100% occupancy the rate rose US$0.50. At 40–80% it did not change. Under 40% it fell US$0.50. sfmta.com
  • Donald Shoup, the professor who wrote the book on parking prices, aims for one or two open spaces on a block. That is about 85% occupancy, or one empty space on a block of eight. If too many spaces are empty the price goes down, and if none are empty it goes up. cato-unbound.org

What people ask

How often does a price that follows demand change?

In San Francisco, meter rates changed about every eight weeks and garage rates every three months. Each change was one fixed step: US$0.25 or US$0.50 an hour. sfmta.com

Did the prices in San Francisco go up?

In the pilot areas the average meter rate fell US$0.11 an hour (4%) and the average garage rate fell US$0.42 an hour (12%), compared with control areas where nothing changed. The 60–80% target was met 31% more often, and the average search for a space was 5 minutes shorter (43%). sfmta.com

What does Shoup propose for the extra money?

Shoup proposes that a city dedicate the revenue to added public services in the neighbourhood where it is collected. His example is Old Pasadena, where over US$1 million a year in meter money went to new public services. cato-unbound.org

Is there software that sets rental prices by demand?

Yes. Beyond sells dynamic pricing for rentals and charges a share of bookings: 1% on its Growth plan and 1.25% on Pro. Its Free plan has no booking fee, and dynamic pricing is not part of it. beyondpricing.com
