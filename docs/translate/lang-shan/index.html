Useful facts

  • The BGN/PCGN romanisation paper says Shan is a Tai language spoken in Burma, with an estimated 3.2 million speakers in Shan State, and that its writing system is derived from Burmese. assets.publishing.service.gov.uk
  • Its table of modern Shan lists 20 consonant characters, and the paper notes 5 tones that the romanisation does not capture. assets.publishing.service.gov.uk
  • Unicode puts the Shan letters, vowel signs and tone marks in its Myanmar block (U+1000–109F), beside Burmese, Mon and Karen letters. unicode.org

What people ask

What language family is Shan?

The BGN/PCGN paper calls it a Tai language spoken in Burma. It says the writing system is derived from Burmese. assets.publishing.service.gov.uk

How many people speak Shan?

The BGN/PCGN paper gives an estimated 3.2 million speakers in Shan State. It takes the figure from the 16th edition of Ethnologue (2009). assets.publishing.service.gov.uk

Can Shan be typed on a computer?

Yes, in Unicode. Its Myanmar block (U+1000–109F) holds Shan letters, vowel signs and tone marks, beside Burmese, Mon and Karen letters. unicode.org
