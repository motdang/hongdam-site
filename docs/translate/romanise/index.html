Good to know

  • In a paper Thailand gave to a UN conference on geographical names, the Royal Institute's transcription writes the sounds of Thai words whatever their spelling; tone marks, silencing marks and vowel length are left out. unstats.un.org
  • The same paper says geographical names are not translated: Ko Si Chang, not Si Chang Island; Maenam Pa Sak, not Pa Sak River; Thanon Tha Phra, not Tha Phra Road. unstats.un.org
  • A proper name that is itself a borrowed word is written as in the original, for example First Class Co.Ltd.; a borrowed word that is only part of a name is written as it sounds in Thai. unstats.un.org
  • Its examples include Nai Somchai Khonching for a person, Wat Duang Khae for a temple and Rongrian Sattri Witthaya for a school. unstats.un.org

What people ask

How are Thai names written in English letters?

In Thailand's paper to a UN conference, the Royal Institute's transcription writes how a word sounds, whatever its Thai spelling. Its example for a person is Nai Somchai Khonching. unstats.un.org

Does the English spelling show tone marks?

Not in the Royal Institute's transcription. The paper says a tone mark, a diacritic including a silencing mark, and vowel length are ignored; the sound is what gets written. unstats.un.org

How are Thai place names written?

The paper says geographical names are not translated, and the general transcription rules apply to the whole name, as in Ko Si Chang and Thanon Tha Phra. unstats.un.org

Are company names with English words changed?

The paper says a borrowed word that is a proper name is written as in the original, for example First Class Co.Ltd. A borrowed word that is only part of a name is written as it sounds in Thai. unstats.un.org
