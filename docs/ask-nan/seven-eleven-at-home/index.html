Why Thailand loves 7-Eleven, and how to heat a กด 5 (press 5) meal at home

ทำไมใครๆ ก็รักเซเว่น และ กด 5 บนซองแปลว่าอะไรเมื่อเวฟที่บ้าน

Why does everyone in Thailand love 7-Eleven so much? And my 7-Eleven rice box says กด 5 on the pack. What does that mean for the microwave in my home?

ทำไมทุกคนในไทยรักเซเว่นนัก แล้วบนซองข้าวกล่องเขียนว่า กด 5 ถ้าเวฟที่บ้านหมายความว่าอย่างไร

Find my microwave time

Nan Peacock, who runs Hongdam

7-Eleven's popularity shows in numbers, and the กด (kot, "press") on the pack names a button on the shop's own microwave. CP All, the company that runs 7-Eleven in Thailand, reports 16,284 stores at the end of June 2026, 200 of them opened in the three months from April to June 1. CP All runs 8,414 itself (about 52 percent); store business partners and sub-area stores run 7,870 (about 48 percent). Of all the stores, 86 percent stand alone and the rest are inside PTT petrol stations 1.

Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai have 487 7-Elevens, and 231 stay open all night

Mot Dang, Hongdam's online directory at motdang.net, lists 487 7-Elevens in Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai. It records 92 with a cash machine and 231 open all night, counted on 18 September 2026 2. 231 of 487 is just under half.

7-Elevens are sacred, with popsicles and beer

On 26 September 2026 I removed a line that called all 7-Elevens the same. My note: "That's not true, 7-Elevens are sacred," and more of the feeling: "hello welcome", popsicles and beer. The One Man Sideshow page has a banner "7-Elevens are sacred", with สวัสดีค่ะ (sawatdee kha, hello), เบียร์เย็น (bia yen, cold beer) and ไอติม (ai tim, popsicles) 2. The Wave It at Home page opens with สวัสดีค่ะ เซเว่นอีเลฟเว่น ยินดีต้อนรับค่ะ (sawatdee kha, seven-eleven, yindi tonrap kha: hello, welcome), and lists popsicles and beer under "No wave. Open the fridge door and take one." 3

กด 5 is a button on the shop's microwave

The number on a ready-meal pack is a program saved on the shop's microwave. Staff press one number and the oven sets its own power and time 3. Postjung, a Thai online forum, described the oven on 23 April 2021 as industrial-grade stainless steel, over 30 kg, with keys numbered 1 to 0 in place of watts (W, the measure of an oven's power), and named Panasonic as the brand 4. Thai reports disagree on the model 3.

The pack names two store oven powers, so one number gives two different times. Times here are minutes and seconds: 1:10 is 1 minute 10 seconds. 7-Eleven's online shop lists the Kurobuta pork curry rice like this: 1,800 W, press 6, 1:10; 1,300 W, press 6, 1:30; and for a home oven, 800 W, 1:45 to 2:00 5.

The number depends on how cold the food is. Sipso rice soup is press 5 at room temperature and press 6 from the fridge, at 2 to 6 °C 6. Frozen meals use 7 or 8 and add a second press 3. Two presses, like 6 and 2, add a short program after the first rather than doubling it: about 15 to 20 seconds in the shop 3.

The home times are calculated, not published

No published table of each code's power and time has been found 3. The home times on the Wave It at Home page are its own calculation, not figures from 7-Eleven. Watts times minutes is the heat the food takes in, and the store times give the heat for each code. A pack line that says "under 1,300 W" is taken to mean 800 W, the middle of what the packs imply, about 650 to 1,050 W. Codes 2 to 6 rise by about 1.46 times per step. Code 1 is estimated from that pattern, with no pack seen. Codes 7 and 8 come from frozen packs. One check: Creator sea bass rice soup lists 800 W, 5:00 and 1,300 W, 3:00, which agree within 3 percent 3.

The page lists its gaps: the AB button appears on one pack, and code 1, and code 8 alone, have no pack with a printed time 3.

At 800 W, press 5 gives 1:30 to 2:00 on the page: the time starts at 1:30 and rises by 15 seconds at a time up to 2:00. Press 6 gives 1:45 to 2:40 3. A pack that prints its own home time can be compared with those ranges. The curry prints 1:45 to 2:00 for code 6 5. Sipso prints 1:00 to 1:30 at 800 W for code 5 6.

The watts are on a sticker inside the microwave door or on the back 3. A small home microwave of 17 to 23 litres uses 700 to 900 W 7.

Press 4 is not 4 minutes

On 10 June 2021 a poster on Pantip, a Thai discussion forum, wrote that a shop clerk had said กด 4 meant 4 minutes. The poster heated a Japanese sweet potato for 4 minutes and burned it. An account named น้องเปาเซเว่น (Nong Pao 7-Eleven) replied that a normal microwave takes about 1 to 1½ minutes, and that the pack has more information 8.
