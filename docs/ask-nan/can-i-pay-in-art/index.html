A portfolio page keeps a maker's work in one place. A share card is the picture that shows when a link is shared on Facebook, LINE, Mastodon or Bluesky. A minisite is a small site of its own, and a reel a short video 1.

To start, the maker sends a photo of the work on LINE, agrees the trade in the chat, and the page goes live. For objects, Mot Dang starts the day the work arrives. For time (photo days, lessons, meals, nights) the page reads "half before we start, half within three months" 1.

Ten more trades sit under "Work, not art", each on its own page at motdang.net/sites/paid-in-…: meals, nights, lessons, print, clothes, repairs, treatments, posts, costumes and rides, at ฿5,999 or ฿9,999 1. The Paid in Meals page reads "3 places left this month" 9.

Van Gogh's paint dealer and doctor held his paintings

Julien Tanguy ran a small paint shop on rue Clauzel in Paris and often took paintings in exchange for the goods he sold 5. In July 1888 Vincent wrote to Theo about a bill from the shop. Vincent replied that Tanguy still held one of his studies. That, he wrote, he owed him if it came to it, but "I don't owe him a sou in money" 6.

Dr Félix Rey was an assistant at the hospital in Arles where Van Gogh was taken. Van Gogh painted his portrait in January 1889 7. The portrait went to the attic, then blocked a hole in the henhouse 7.

Jo van Gogh-Bonger sold the rest

Jo van Gogh-Bonger (1862 to 1925) was married to Theo, Vincent's brother. Vincent died, and Theo died six months later, leaving Jo with their son and a sizeable art collection 3. From 1891 to 1925 she sold at least 192 of Vincent's paintings and 55 works on paper, to private buyers and influential dealers, organised exhibitions in the Netherlands and abroad, and loaned work to museums 34. In 1905 she united 484 works at the Stedelijk Museum in Amsterdam, and in 1914 she published Vincent's letters to Theo 13. The works she chose not to sell passed to her son and now form the heart of the Van Gogh Museum, which opened in 1973 3.
