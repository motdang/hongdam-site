Afraid of the bum gun? Eight steps to a clean bottom and a dry floor

กลัวสายฉีดชำระ? 8 ขั้นตอน ก้นสะอาด พื้นไม่เปียก

Dear Nan, Thai bathrooms have a small hose next to the toilet. The first time I used one, I sprayed the floor, my trousers and the wall. How do I use it without a flood? — Wet Trousers in Chiang Mai

ถึงแนน ห้องน้ำในไทยมีสายฉีดเล็ก ๆ อยู่ข้างโถส้วม ครั้งแรกที่ใช้ ฉันฉีดโดนพื้น โดนกางเกง แล้วก็โดนผนัง จะใช้ยังไงไม่ให้น้ำท่วมห้องน้ำ — กางเกงเปียกที่เชียงใหม่

Dear Wet Trousers,

Your floor can stay dry. It takes one test squirt into the bowl and a light squeeze. The hose is สายฉีดชำระ (sai chit chamra, "spray hose for cleansing"). In English, people call it a bum gun. Its head is the spray end, and it stays about a hand's width from you. The lever under the head sets the force. A quarter squeeze gives a soft spray 1.

On 11 October 2026 I asked for a "cleverly illustrated minisite" on how to use one, and said to "add lots of toys". A minisite is a small website. The result has a bathroom you can tap, a game, and a toy that takes the gun apart 1. Its squeeze toy shows the range. Too soft, and the water dribbles on the floor. Too hard, and it reaches the gecko on the ceiling. A gecko is a small house lizard 1.

Eight steps to a dry floor

  1. The small valve on the wall opens. It feeds only the gun.
  2. One short squirt goes into the toilet bowl. It shows how hard this gun sprays, and how warm the water is.
  3. The gun reaches in from behind, or down between the knees. People do both.
  4. The squeeze is gentle. The head stays about a hand's width away.
  5. The spray moves from front to back, two or three times.
  6. The gun goes back on its holder.
  7. A few sheets of paper pat the skin dry.
  8. The valve on the wall closes.

Bangkok Biznews, a Thai news site, quotes a how-to guide written by foreigners. It also starts with a test squirt, because pressure and water temperature differ from place to place 2.

The tap on the wall makes the spray soft

The stop valve is the small tap on the pipe that feeds the gun. HomeGuru is the advice section of HomePro, a Thai home shop. It describes sprays so hard that people jump at a public or unfamiliar toilet. Its answer is the stop valve. Turning it sets the pressure that reaches the head 3. On the Mot Dang page, a half-open valve makes a strong spray soft 1.

A HANG spray set at HomePro costs ฿169, checked on 24 October 2026 4. It lists a flow of up to 6 litres a minute and a pressure of up to 1 bar. A bar is a unit of pressure, a little below the average air pressure at sea level 5. Full sets in the page's price table cost ฿169 to ฿615, checked on 10 October 2026 1.

Bin or bowl: where the paper goes

A sign on the wall tells which to use, the bin or the bowl. Many Thai toilets keep a bin for used paper 1. In 2009 the Thai newspaper Manager tested tissue brands. Two-ply paper, with two layers, formed lumps when wet. The newspaper wrote that paper does not belong in the toilet or the drains 6. In the page's bin-or-bowl game, wet wipes, sanitary pads, cotton buds, plastic and hair go in the bin. A scoop of water from a dipper goes in the bowl 1.

Squat toilets and the dipper

In a 2010 survey of Thai homes, 86.0% had a squat toilet, a pan set in the floor. Another 10.1% had a sitting toilet, and 3.1% had both 7. Wikipedia says "the 'bum gun' is ubiquitous" in Thailand, which means it is found everywhere 8. A Thai bathroom usually also has a plastic dipper, ขัน (khan), used for bathing 8. A squat pan with no water tank is flushed by pouring water: ส้วมราดน้ำ (suam rat nam). The page's toy counts three dipperfuls 1.

A little history

A Thai firm with the brand HANG says it began making spray sets in 1972. It says it has made over 50 million 2. That is the company's own claim. Sanook, a Thai news site, repeats it and adds that Thailand did not invent the hose 9. In 1968 Oras, a Finnish firm, made a hand shower for the toilet called the Bidetta 10.

The names: สายฉีดก้น (sai chit kon, "bottom spray hose") is a joking name 9. ที่ฉีดตูด (thi chit tut) is slang 11. "Bum gun" is English slang, and Wiktionary also lists "health faucet" 11. The word ชำระ (chamra) means to cleanse. Thai uses the same word for paying a bill: ชำระเงิน (chamra ngoen) 1.

On 15 August 2019 a member of parliament complained about Thailand's new parliament building. Its toilets had no สายฉีดชำระ. The Speaker of the House went to look at them 12.

Toilet paper, country by country

A person in the United States uses 141 rolls of toilet paper a year. In the United Kingdom it is 127, in Japan 91 and in France 71. Reuters reported these figures in 2019, from a report by two environmental groups 13. A Thai figure in rolls was not found.

A hose that drips

HomeGuru lists five causes of a leak: a cracked head, a broken lever, a loose joint, a leaking hose, or water pressure that is too high. Pressure is the most common. It happens in dorms, condos and houses with a strong pump, where the head shakes in use. For that case the fix is the stop valve again. It is turned down, and closed when the gun is not used for a long time 3.

One test squirt, one soft squeeze, and the gecko on the ceiling stays dry.
