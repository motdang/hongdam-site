Good to know

  • Khwan is the life essence a person is born with, and Lanna counts 32. A fright, an illness, a fall or a long journey can make it wander. The khwan rite calls it back. motdang.net
  • A mo khwan calls the khwan: usually an older man who was ordained as a monk and left the robe. He reads the call over the finished tray, then ties white cotton thread on the wrists, left first. The thread stays on for about 3 days, until it greys. motdang.net
  • The northern khwan tray, khan bai si, is a silver or lacquer bowl on a footed stand. It holds tiers of banana leaf folded into points, a ball of steamed sticky rice, a peeled boiled egg, a banana, flowers, incense, candles and white cotton thread for the wrists. motdang.net
  • A 2023 article in the Journal of Humanities of Naresuan University defines su khwan as calling the khwan back into the patient's body. It says northern and Isan Thai people use the rite to care for, prevent and treat illness of body and mind. so03.tci-thaijo.org
  • Mot Dang's page gives a rule for the thread: tie the left hand, the khwan comes; tie the right hand, the khwan stays. The thread is untied, not cut. motdang.net

What people ask

What happens at the ceremony?

Mot Dang's page says the mo khwan first honours his teachers. He then chants the call with a little Pali, names the places each khwan may have wandered and asks them to return. Guests sit before the tray with their palms joined. The call takes 10 to 15 minutes. motdang.net

How long does the thread stay on?

Mot Dang's page says about 3 days, until the thread greys. It says the thread is untied rather than cut. motdang.net

Is a bai si part of a northern Thai wedding?

TAT News describes the northern style: after the wedding, both parties host a 'Bai Sri' ritual in which the bride's and groom's hands are tied. Coins are placed in the 'Bai Sri' bowls for prosperity. tatnews.org

Is the Lao baci a similar ceremony?

The Laotian Times, reprinted by The Star on 12 May 2023, describes the baci as a ritual Lao people have practised for thousands of years. It gives the name sou khuan, meaning calling of the soul, and says white cotton strings are tied on wrists while blessings are prayed. thestar.com.my
